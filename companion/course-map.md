# Course map

This page maps *Cybersecurity in the Gulf* to a fourteen week semester course. The whole book supports a semester; Part I can also be taught alone as a short course of four to six sessions, and Parts III and IV as a second semester for specialists.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of Part I a student should be able to:

1. Describe the Gulf cyber theatre, its dependency stack and its six amplifiers, and apply them to a risk assessment.
2. Classify adversaries and reconstruct a landmark incident in terms of techniques and controls.
3. Describe the governance architecture of each GCC state and the regional structures that connect them.
4. Inventory the obligations that bind a named organisation and apply the crosswalk method to them.
5. Build an incident notification register and defend its content against the current instruments.
6. Produce a threat model and a risk register for a regional organisation that assume a state aligned adversary.
7. Design identity, authentication and third party access that resist the region's fraud patterns and integrate with a national identity platform.
8. Produce a cryptographic inventory and a post quantum migration priority list.
9. Place a classified workload in the cloud under the residency and key control rules of a named state.
10. Specify security requirements for a bought application and model the trust in an open banking flow.
11. Build a data map and decide a cross border transfer under the laws of a named state.
12. Define zones, conduits and controls for a plant and run an exercise on a cyber range.
13. Map a bank's critical service, set an impact tolerance and design the test that proves it.
14. Specify secure by design and procurement requirements for a national digital service.
15. Engineer detection against the regional threat model, run a brand protection pipeline and lead a tabletop exercise.
16. Design calendar driven awareness, a talent plan and the lawful boundary for research.
17. Govern the adoption of artificial intelligence and other emerging technology, and assess a national strategy against the five pillars and the resilience test.

## Assessment guidance

The exercises are designed so that answers date quickly: a crosswalk built against last year's instrument is wrong this year. Assessors should reward students who locate the current instrument, cite its version and record where it differs from the book. Submitting a correction to the book's companion repository or to one of the open source tools is a legitimate graded activity.

## Reading paths for short courses

- **Executive briefing (half day)**: Chapter 1, the pattern section of Chapter 2, the comparison table in Chapter 3 and the crosswalk method in Chapter 4.
- **Regulator induction (two days)**: Chapters 3 and 4 in full, Appendix A, and Exercises 3.1 and 4.1.
- **Practitioner workshop (two days)**: Chapters 2 and 4, Exercises 2.1 and 4.3, with the open source companions in Appendix C.
