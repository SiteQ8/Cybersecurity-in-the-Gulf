# Course map

This page maps *Cybersecurity in the Gulf* to a fourteen week semester course. Part I, contained in the 2026 edition, supports the first unit of the course and can also be taught as a short course of four to six sessions. Chapters beyond Part I are listed as planned and will appear in later editions; lecturers can substitute readings from the sources listed at the end of each chapter until then.

## Learning outcomes for Part I

By the end of Part I a student should be able to:

1. Describe the Gulf cyber theatre, its dependency stack and its six amplifiers, and apply them to a risk assessment.
2. Classify adversaries and reconstruct a landmark incident in terms of techniques and controls.
3. Describe the governance architecture of each GCC state and the regional structures that connect them.
4. Inventory the obligations that bind a named organisation and apply the crosswalk method to them.
5. Build an incident notification register and defend its content against the current instruments.

## Assessment guidance

The exercises are designed so that answers date quickly: a crosswalk built against last year's instrument is wrong this year. Assessors should reward students who locate the current instrument, cite its version and record where it differs from the book. Submitting a correction to the book's companion repository or to one of the open source tools is a legitimate graded activity.

## Reading paths for short courses

- **Executive briefing (half day)**: Chapter 1, the pattern section of Chapter 2, the comparison table in Chapter 3 and the crosswalk method in Chapter 4.
- **Regulator induction (two days)**: Chapters 3 and 4 in full, Appendix A, and Exercises 3.1 and 4.1.
- **Practitioner workshop (two days)**: Chapters 2 and 4, Exercises 2.1 and 4.3, with the open source companions in Appendix C.
